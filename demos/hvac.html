How a visit works

Four steps, in this order, with the prices in writing before any work starts.

  1. Request

    Tell us what the system is doing and which window suits you.

  2. Confirm

    We call to confirm a time window and ask a few questions about the equipment.

  3. Diagnose and explain

    The technician finds the cause, then lays out the options and prices in writing before work starts.

  4. Fix or schedule

    Small repairs can happen on the visit. Larger jobs get a day on the calendar.

Questions we hear

Plain answers to the questions homeowners ask before they book.

How do I know whether to repair or replace?

A furnace near the end of its expected life, or one that has needed several repairs, usually tips toward replacement. A technician can compare the repair quote with the cost of a new system, with your equipment in front of them.

What does a tune-up cover?

Cleaning key parts, checking safety controls, measuring airflow and temperatures, and noting anything that looks worn. You get the findings in writing.

How soon can a technician come out?

That depends on the season and the schedule that day. We confirm a window by phone when you call or send a request, and we ask first whether your system is out.

Should I keep running the system while I wait?

If you smell gas or a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, leave the house and call 911 or your gas utility from outside. For a system that is icing up or making loud noises, turning it off can prevent more damage. Otherwise, follow what the technician tells you on the phone.

What affects the cost of a new installation?

Home size, equipment type and efficiency, ductwork condition, and local permits. Permit and inspection rules differ between Denver and the surrounding cities, and the estimate lists each so you can compare options.

Can I get a written estimate?

Yes. Estimates for repairs and replacements are written out with options before work begins, so you can decide at your own pace.
