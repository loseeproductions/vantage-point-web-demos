Services

Repairs, in writing

Tap a service to see what a Phoenix visit includes and what moves the price. Costs depend on the vehicle, the parts and the time, so the price arrives as a written estimate after inspection.

01

Diagnostics

Warning lights, noises, odd smells, rough running

A visit includes

Scan for stored codes, a road test when it is safe, and a visual check of the related system. You get the code, what it points to, and what was ruled out.

Affects cost

How long it takes to trace the cause, and whether the symptom comes and goes.

02

Brakes

Squeal, grinding, soft or pulsing pedal

A visit includes

Pads, rotors, calipers, lines and fluid checked, with measurements written down next to the minimum for your vehicle.

Affects cost

Pads only or pads and rotors, part quality, and whether calipers or lines need work.

03

Oil and maintenance

Oil, filters, fluids, belts, tires, scheduled service

A visit includes

The service your maker schedules for your mileage, plus a short look over belts, hoses, fluids and tires.

Affects cost

Oil type and capacity, filter choice, and which scheduled items are due.

04

Engine

Misfires, leaks, overheating, timing

A visit includes

The shop works to confirm the cause before quoting repair, so you are not paying to swap parts on a guess.

Affects cost

Engine layout, how much has to come apart to reach the part, and the part itself.

05

Transmission

Slipping, hard shifts, fluid service

A visit includes

Fluid condition, codes, and a road test. Service or repair options are written out with what each does and does not fix.

Affects cost

Fluid or repair, the transmission type, and whether other parts are involved.

06

AC and heating

Weak cooling, no heat, odd smells

A visit includes

Pressure and leak check, compressor and fan operation, and cabin filter condition.

Affects cost

Where a leak is, which refrigerant the system uses, and the parts needed.

07

Electrical and battery

Slow starts, dead batteries, warning lights

A visit includes

Battery and charging test, starter draw, and a check for something drawing power while parked.

Affects cost

Battery size, how hard the fault is to trace, and wiring access.

08

Steering and suspension

Pulling, vibration, clunks over bumps

A visit includes

Joints, bushings, shocks and tires inspected on the lift, with wear shown to you.

Affects cost

Which parts are worn, whether an alignment follows, and part brand.

How a visit goes

Five steps, in writing

  1. Request

    Tell the shop what you notice. A call, a text or the form all reach the same place.

    You see: a confirmation of your vehicle and concern.

  2. Drop off

    Hand over the keys, or wait if the visit is short. The shop writes down your concern in your words.

    You see: your concern on the work order.

  3. Inspection

    A technician checks the system and logs findings, with photos where they help.

    You see: a report sorted Now, Soon, Watch.

  4. You approve

    Each line has its own price. Approve some, defer some. Work starts only on approved lines.

    You see: an itemized written estimate.

  5. Pickup

    Finished work is compared to the estimate. Old parts are set aside if you asked to see them.

    You see: the invoice next to the estimate.

Questions

Before you call

Do you start work before I approve?

No. Inspection comes first, then a written estimate, then work only on the lines you approve (example policy).

My check engine light is on. What happens at the visit?

The shop reads the stored codes, which point to a system and not always to a single part. Then comes a road test when it is safe and a look at the related parts. You leave with what was found and what it points to, and nothing is repaired until you approve a line.

Why do AC and batteries struggle in Phoenix summers?

Heat is hard on batteries, belts, hoses and AC systems, so a Phoenix shop would check those first when you report weak cooling or a slow start. A written inspection shows what tested fine and what is wearing.

Is there a warranty on repairs?

Warranty terms go here, in writing (example text: the shop sets its own terms).

How is the price decided?

By the parts, the labor time for that job and any shop fees, all written out per line. Example: the shop would list accepted cards and any financing here. Placeholder prices on this site are labeled as examples.

Can I keep my old parts?

Ask when you drop off and they can be set aside for you. Some parts, like those returned for credit, may need to go back to the supplier.
